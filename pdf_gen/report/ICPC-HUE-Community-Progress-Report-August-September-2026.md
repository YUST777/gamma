# ICPC HUE Community Progress Report

**August – September 2026**  
**DCC 2026, ECPC participation, community development, and the 2026/2027 season**

## Executive overview

During the 2026 competitive programming season, ICPC HUE moved from preparation to organized execution. The community first helped establish the DCC contest in Damietta as a practical preparation opportunity, then supported Horus University students through the ECPC qualification stage. It coordinated travel and accommodation, created opportunities for team building, and established the foundations of a stronger 2026/2027 operating model.

The qualification experience gave the community a clearer understanding of what students need before a major contest and what a sustainable training structure must provide afterward. The result is a more focused model built around four committees, mentor follow-up, instructor development, a dedicated platform, and partnerships that extend beyond Horus University.

- **4 community committees:** instructors, mentors, media, and operations.

## DCC 2026 and competitive programming in Damietta

Before the ECPC qualification stage, ICPC HUE helped host and present the first edition of the DCC Competitive Programming Contest in Damietta in 2026, organized in cooperation with ACPC Club Damietta University. The event was designed to be a first practical step for competitive programming in Damietta and to give students realistic preparation for ECPC.

The contest was delivered in two phases: online qualification followed by an offline final. More than one hundred contestants participated from five communities:

- ICPC MNU
- ICPC HUE
- ICPC DELTA
- ACPC DU
- ACPC NDETI

Members of the ICPC HUE community also participated in the competition and achieved **ninth place in the DCC online qualification**. This result reflected the community’s active contribution as both an organizer and a competitor, while showing the value of DCC as an early competitive test before ECPC.

ICPC HUE also contributed to the event’s digital infrastructure. Community member **Yousef Mohamed** developed [dcchub.xyz](https://dcchub.xyz), the official DCC website, giving the competition a dedicated platform for its public information and online presence.

The event was supported by Dr. Hossam El-Din Mostafa, Dean of the Faculty of Artificial Intelligence, and held under the supervision of Dr. Basma Mostafa, Cybersecurity Program Coordinator, and Dr. Yasser El-Awady, Robotics and Intelligent Systems Program Coordinator.

ICPC HUE also recognizes the contribution of Eng. Fatma Magdy, Eng. Sara Lotfy, and Eng. Raafat El-Moneer, whose support helped the community deliver the event and guide students throughout the experience. DCC became more than a single contest: it created relationships that later supported cross-community training and instructor development.

## ECPC preparation and participation

Approximately one month before the ECPC qualification stage, ICPC HUE began preparing students and organizing the practical requirements of participation. The community reviewed the advanced topics students needed, completed the official participation procedures, arranged accommodation, and supported the teams throughout the travel and competition schedule.

The delegation travelled on August 18. After arriving, students were accommodated and took part in community activities,  The following day, the teams attended the Arab Academy for Science, Technology and Maritime Transport, where they completed the practice contest, received their team shirts, and prepared for the official qualification contest.

The qualification contest lasted several hours and included twelve problems. Most teams solved at least three problems, while the leading team solved four and came within one problem of advancing to the next stage. These results exceeded the community’s expectations and provided a concrete basis for improving next season’s preparation plan.

The competition also clarified the full progression ahead of the students: the qualification stage leads to ECPC, then to ACPC, and ultimately to the ICPC World Finals. ICPC HUE is using this experience to build earlier preparation, stronger follow-up, and more consistent practice before the next qualification cycle.

- **3 or more problems** solved by most participating teams.
- **4 problems** solved by the leading team.

## Preparing the 2026/2027 season

After the ECPC qualification period, ICPC HUE immediately began preparing for the 2026/2027 season. The community published a sequence of social media updates covering the participating teams, competition photos, the travel experience, and student activities. Together, this content generated approximately 43,000 views on Facebook and helped introduce the community to a wider group of students.

Three additional videos were prepared to attract new members and communicate the community’s work. The content introduced competitive programming, documented the community’s activities, and presented social media work created by students. This communication plan helped turn the competition experience into an ongoing recruitment and engagement process rather than a one-time event.

The main organizational objective for the new season was to establish clear committees with defined responsibilities. The community’s experience during the competition made it possible to identify where preparation, follow-up, communication, and event organization needed to become more systematic.

## Community committees

### Instructors

Instructors deliver technical sessions, prepare independently, solve problems accurately, communicate with patience, and maintain academic honesty even when a topic requires further study. They are expected to have strong problem-solving experience and the ability to engage students during each session.

### Mentors

Mentors follow teams continuously, check in when students fall behind, monitor progress and wellbeing, and help students maintain a consistent solving routine. Their role is to make sure that students are not only assigned tasks, but are also supported while working through them.

### Media

The media committee produces presentations, videos, and social media posts that document the community’s work and attract new students. New members with experience in Adobe After Effects and Premiere Pro have already created independent videos for the community.

### Operations Committee

The Operations Committee coordinates offline activities, logistics, event flow, materials, and communication. It forms the operational backbone of the community’s student activities.

## Leadership development and recruitment

To build the next generation of instructors and mentors, ICPC HUE interviewed the top five teams from the ECPC qualification stage. Four of the selected teams were from the Faculty of Artificial Intelligence and one was from Engineering. Candidates were asked to study topics such as STL, vectors, and other advanced subjects over a five-day period, then explain what they had learned to the community. This process evaluated not only technical understanding, but also preparation, clarity, patience, and the ability to support other students.

The interviews were supported by Eng. Fatma, Eng. Sara, and Eng. Raafat. A core community principle was maintained throughout the process: no member is forced into a particular committee. Each student chooses the role in which they can contribute most effectively, whether that is instruction, mentoring, media, or operations.

To widen participation, ICPC HUE published a **Hire Up** recruitment announcement for Horus University students with experience in instruction, mentoring, operations, or media. Applications were collected through the community platform at [icpchue.com/job](https://icpchue.com/job).

## The ICPC HUE platform and mentoring model

The ICPC HUE platform was prepared for the new season as a central space for recruitment, training, and follow-up. The mentor dashboard now gives mentors a structured view of student progress, while students record the sheets and problems they solve each day. This daily record supports self-reflection, continuity, and a more accurate understanding of each student’s development over time.

The platform also allows the community to review submitted solutions and training activity so that mentors can identify gaps early, provide targeted feedback, and support an honest training environment. The goal is not simply to count solved problems; it is to understand the process behind each student’s progress and help teams build reliable habits before the next competition.

By connecting students, mentors, instructors, and community leadership in one system, ICPC HUE is moving from informal follow-up to a repeatable training workflow. This structure will make it easier to compare progress, recognize students who need support, and prepare teams consistently throughout the season.

## Strategic partnerships and knowledge exchange

The DCC experience opened the door to strategic relationships with other university communities. These partnerships are designed to strengthen training quality, share practical experience, and create a broader competitive programming network for students in the region.

- **ACPC Club Damietta University:** collaboration around DCC and the development of competitive programming activities in Damietta.
- **ICPC PSU:** a connection with a community that included a team earning a bronze medal in ECPC, creating an opportunity for experienced competitors to support ICPC HUE students.
- **ICPC Assiut University Community:** cooperation on instructor development and training, drawing on one of the larger and more experienced student communities in the field.

These relationships are important because they give ICPC HUE access to experience beyond its own current teams. They also position the community as an active contributor to the wider student competitive programming ecosystem rather than an isolated university activity.

## Conclusion and next objectives

The 2026 ECPC cycle proved that ICPC HUE can prepare teams, coordinate a major student experience, organize a competitive programming event, and build relationships beyond Horus University. More importantly, it showed where the community can improve: earlier preparation, stronger daily follow-up, clearer leadership roles, and a more connected training system.

For the 2026/2027 season, ICPC HUE will focus on expanding the instructor and mentor teams, using the platform consistently, strengthening cooperation with partner communities, and preparing a larger number of students for the next ECPC qualification cycle. The long-term objective remains clear: establish a sustainable competitive programming culture at Horus University and create a reliable path from the first problem solved to the highest levels of collegiate competition.

The next cycle will therefore prioritize four practical actions:

1. Expand the instructor and mentor pipeline so students receive consistent technical and personal follow-up.
2. Use the ICPC HUE platform as the daily record for training activity, progress review, and targeted support.
3. Continue knowledge exchange with partner communities through joint training and instructor development.
4. Start structured preparation earlier so more Horus University teams can reach the next ECPC stage.

ICPC HUE is continuing to build that path one student, one team, and one well-organized training cycle at a time.
