01 · Who We Are
About the Community

Who we are, and why this work deserves support

ICPC HUE is a student community founded in the Faculty of AI that serves all faculties of Horus University. We train students over a full season (one academic year) to compete in the ICPC.

The idea is not new: after ECPC in Egypt asked every university to form a student team to train its students, almost every participating university now has a community like ours. We are Horus University's community, active since November 2025, and we want Horus to hold a permanent place among these universities.

What is the ICPC?

The largest and oldest university programming contest in the world. Students compete in teams of 3 from the same university, sharing one computer, to solve a set of programming problems in 5 hours. The team that solves the most problems in the least time wins. Teams advance through these stages:

University QualifiersTeams from the same university compete first
→
ECPCThe Egyptian contest, held each August
→
ACPCArab countries and Africa
→
World FinalsICPC World Finals

What do we study? Problem Solving

We take a logic problem and turn it into a correct, fast program. A simple example: you have 4 sticks of different lengths. Can you form a square with them? Given the lengths 8 6 11 12, the answer is no, because the sides are unequal. Given 8 8 8 8, the answer is yes.

As students progress, problems get harder and require algorithms and data structures such as STL, binary search, dynamic programming and graphs, and the solution must run in a fraction of a second on huge inputs.

// Input: 8 8 8 8
read a, b, c, d
print(a == b && b == c && c == d)
// Output: true

Why does it matter?

For the student
  • A filter for employers: the contest shows who truly codes well and understands hard topics.
  • A path to a job: interviews at companies such as Noon and the global FAANG companies are built on Problem Solving questions, which are hard to pass without training.
  • A stronger CV: competing and working in committees is real experience for the CV.
For the university
  • Horus in the results: the university's name appears in the contest rankings alongside every university in Egypt.
  • In-demand graduates: students with a strong foundation in programming and logical thinking, which the job market seeks.
  • Sustainable activity: today's contestants become tomorrow's instructors, passing experience from one cohort to the next.

02 · How We Work
A fixed system repeated every season

How we train, and how we organize ourselves

We follow the same plan every year: a student starts from zero and ends up on a team ready for the ECPC qualifiers.

The weekly training cycle

Teaching sessionOnline or offline
→
Problem sheetOn the platform after each session
→
Mentor follow-upDaily log and weekly review
→
Up SolveWe solve the hard ones together and share experience

Sessions can reach two per week at busy times. Training runs through the first and second terms and the summer. Topics change by stage and student level, with regular practice contests followed by team-formation contests.

Our goals

  • Train our registered students all season so they reach the contest ready.
  • Help them access job opportunities and build a CV through our activities.
  • Build training tools for Horus University not available at other universities.
  • Increase the number of Horus teams reaching the advanced ECPC stages each year.

Training levels

  • Level 0: programming language basics for complete beginners.
  • Level 1: intro to competitive programming: STL, binary search and two pointers.
  • Level 2: advanced contest-level algorithms and data structures.
  • Students are grouped by level and progress.

4 committees, each member chooses their own

No member is assigned to a committee. Each student picks the role that fits their skills, so they stay more committed.

1 · Instructors
They teach the weekly sessions, solve problems step by step and prepare the sheets. They need deep understanding, academic integrity and patience with beginners.
2 · Mentors
Each mentor follows a small group closely, contacts students who fall behind, and tracks their progress and status so they keep solving.
3 · Media
Presentations, videos (Premiere and After Effects) and posts that document student achievements and attract new members.
4 · Organizing Committee
Booking labs with university administration, running offline events and contests, and handling printing and supplies. It currently has 11 members.

How we formed the committees: in two phases

Phase 1: the technical core. We interviewed the top 5 teams from the ECPC qualifiers (4 from AI and one from Engineering). Each candidate studied a topic such as STL in 5 days and then taught it to the community, supervised by Eng. Fatma Magdy, Eng. Sara Lotfy and Eng. Raafat Mohamed.
Phase 2: the “Hire Up” campaign. We opened applications to all Horus students via icpchue.com/job for Media and Organizing, and received 50 applicants.

09 · Platform (1/4): Solving Environment
Why did we need our own platform?

The icpchue.com platform: solving problems every student faced

Problem: In most communities, sessions are recorded and posted a week later. Students jump between YouTube, Telegram groups and the crowded Codeforces site, and the instructor cannot tell who solved and who stopped. Solution: One platform for explanations, problems, editor, judging and follow-up, built 100% by community students, with open-source code on GitHub. We know of no other Egyptian university with its own training platform like this.

+800registered studentsfrom the Faculty of AI
+50daily active studentssolving on the platform
650problems in 3 levelsa full one-season curriculum
263trainees in the mentor dashboard154 of them active (October 2026)
Student dashboard: the first thing they see on login.

Student Dashboard

  • Students lose track of where they stopped: the current sheet, its progress and a "Continue solving" button at the top.
  • Dropping off goes unnoticed: a 3-month submission heatmap and a daily streak counter.
  • Progress is scattered across sites: platform solves and Codeforces rating in one place.

Problem Page: everything on one screen

"Area of a Circle" from the first sheet: statement and examples on the right, editor on the left.
  • Switching tabs breaks focus: the statement and a VS Code-grade editor on one screen, supporting C++, Python, Java and C.
  • Waiting in judge queues: a Judge0 judge on the community server runs code instantly and returns AC / WA / TLE / RE.
  • "Wrong Answer" with no explanation: input, expected output and the student's output side by side, so they find the bug themselves.
  • Code lost between devices: every attempt auto-saved to the student's account.
  • Beginners struggle with English: in-page hints and explanations in Arabic.

10 · Platform (2/4): Curriculum and Sessions
A clear path from the first line of code

650 ordered problems, and every session in one place

Problem: New students don't know where to start or what to solve after each session. Online problems are many and unordered, so they quit within weeks. Solution: A curriculum split into levels, each level into sheets, each sheet tied to a session, plus a roadmap showing where the student stands at any moment.

The three levels: Beginners, Level 1, and advanced Level 2.
"Data Types and Conditions" sheet: 26 problems and the student's progress bar.
LevelProblemsTopics (roadmap prepared with the Assiut ICPC community)
Level 0: Beginners249 in 10 sheetsData types and conditions, loops, arrays, strings, functions, math and geometry, recursion, and general problems at three difficulty tiers.
Level 1: Intermediate226 in 8 sheetsTime complexity, STL containers and sorting, binary search and two pointers, sliding window, Bitmask, number theory, and prefix sums.
Level 2: Advanced171 problemsAdvanced contest-level data structures and algorithms.
Roadmap: the student's progress in each topic.
STL 1 session: the video inside the platform with a topic description.
Missed sessions used to be lost
A student who missed a session waited a week for it to be posted in a group. Now 12 recorded sessions across 3 camps live on the platform, each with its sheet on the same day.
Videos were heavy on weak connections
In September 2026 we moved all sessions to YouTube (unlisted), played from inside the platform, so they load fast on any connection.

11 · Platform (3/4): Leaderboard and Achievements
Keeping students going

Leaderboard and achievements: training as a game

Problem: The hardest part of competitive programming is consistency. Over 500 students registered with us, only 21 made it to the qualifiers, and most who stopped lost motivation in the first weeks. Solution: A full motivation layer: rankings that update with every problem, badges to earn, and real-world prizes.

Leaderboard: students ranked by accepted solves in training sheets, with a second tab for Codeforces rating (names hidden).
Achievements: welcome badge, Level 0 completed, first sheet solved, and +500 rating.
Daily streak and consistency heatmap on the student dashboard.
Students don't know how they rank among peers
Two leaderboards: platform sheets and global Codeforces rating. Since 24 Dec 2025 we post the weekly leaders.
Competition without prizes fades fast
Since 15 Mar 2026, first place wins an ICPC HUE T-shirt. Mostafa Kamal, March's champion, won it (3 Apr).
Digital achievements go unseen
Each achievement unlocked earns a laptop sticker; the first batch was handed out on Orientation Day, 12 Mar.
Dropping off starts with one day
A streak counter and consistency heatmap reveal any gap, so students return before falling behind.

16 · What We Need
Support Requests for the 2026/2027 Season

7 Specific Requests to Help Us Double Our Results

Training, the platform, and follow-up are run entirely by the community team. What we need from the University are specific facilities we cannot provide ourselves. For each request: the current situation, exactly what we ask, and the expected result.

1
A Fixed Hall or Lab for Sessions Now: We search for an available room every week, so sessions get cancelled or moved online.
We ask: A hall or lab reserved for us for two hours weekly, at a fixed time throughout the term.
Result: Students know where and when to attend, so they stay committed.
2
A Lab Equipped for Contests Now: Lab computers have no programming environments, so we ask students to bring laptops.
We ask: Installing C++, Python, and a code editor, and unblocking icpchue.com and Codeforces on the University network.
Result: On-campus contests under the same conditions as ECPC.
3
Attendance Excuses for Events Now: Events and contests clash with lectures and sections, forcing students to choose.
We ask: An official absence excuse for students in approved events, based on a name list we send in advance.
Result: Students take part without losing academic attendance.
4
5 Minutes for Announcements in Lectures Now: Many students learn about the community or the contest only when it is too late.
We ask: Faculty approval for 5 minutes at the start of one lecture per cohort, once at the start of each term.
Result: The information reaches all students, not only our page followers.
5
Coordination with the Official Faculty Page Now: Announcements of our activities are sometimes posted late or with details needing correction.
We ask: A faculty contact person to whom we send the reviewed announcement and details one week before each event.
Result: Official announcements on time and with accurate information.
6
Official Recognition for Students Now: Contestants and committee members work all year without any recognition from the University.
We ask: Certificates accredited by the University Presidency, activity credits, or consideration of their absences.
Result: Students stay on, and others see that effort is valued.
7
An Activity Budget Now: There is no budget allocated to the community.
We ask: Support for four items: (1) ECPC team registration fees, 1,500 EGP per team, as other universities such as Damietta do. (2) Printing, certificates, T-shirts, and internal contest prizes. (3) Hospitality at events. (4) Platform hosting and domain.
Result: More teams at ECPC, and events that reflect the University's reputation.
Thank you for your support. Together, we raise Horus University's name at ECPC, ACPC, and ICPC.Page 17 of 19
